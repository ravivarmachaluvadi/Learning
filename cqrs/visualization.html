It never reaches zero. Tightening the window from 2 s to streaming takes the oversell from 46.7 to 0.33 — 140× better, still not safe. What the reports do to checkout

Log scale. The bar you care about is checkout's p99 — the customer's experience of pressing Place Order.

shared pool of 8 checkout alone on 6
The mechanism is not CPU. An 800 ms report occupies a worker for 800 ms. A 5 ms write arriving when several workers hold reports waits behind them — head-of-line blocking. At 4 reports/s checkout's p99 is 406 ms, about half of one report's service time, which is exactly the average wait behind a job you are stuck behind.

The two arrangements, side by side

Giving checkout 25% fewer workers made it 69× faster, because it stopped sharing a queue with work 160× larger. This is the pooling result from your load-balancing lab read backwards: pool identical work, separate dissimilar work. It is also the first reason to split reads from writes — before shape, before scale.

Rebuild time against live load

The vertical asymptote is at λ = μ. To the right of it there is no rebuild time, because the rebuild never finishes.

rebuild time your current λ λ = μ, never finishes

The same rebuild, at different live loads

31 hours on a quiet system, 6.6 days at 82% utilisation — which most teams would call healthy. Past ρ = 1 you have a read model you can never recreate, which means a read-side schema change you can never make. Compute this number quarterly, before you need it, not during the incident.
The three real answers when the number is too big: build the new version alongside the live one and cut over (blue/green); replay with far more workers than the live projector uses — safe because the ordering constraint is only per key, which is why §10.4's keying decision pays off twice; or reduce H with snapshots or a shorter retention.
Every figure on this page comes from the simulator below, which is the same engine the lab's README.md quotes — the document and this page agree digit for digit. The model: commands commit as a Poisson process; a projector reads after its checkpoint, applies each event, and commits the checkpoint, with the applies and the checkpoint sharing one read-side transaction so a batch becomes visible at one instant. It is a model of the mechanism, not a measurement of any real database.
