Nothing Is Ever Erased

A database does not change a row. It writes a new copy and notes when the old one stopped being true — which is how a forty-minute report and a one-second payment can share a table without waiting for each other. The price is that somebody must come back later and throw the old copies away, and they are only allowed to throw away what no reader could still want. This page shows both halves happening, and lets you stop the second one dead.

A model of PostgreSQL's heap. Pages hold 6 tuple slots instead of ~35, transaction IDs are 6-bit so you can watch them wrap, and vacuum's four phases are separate steps so you can walk through them. The rules are PostgreSQL's; the sizes are shrunk to fit a screen. One deliberate simplification: at fillfactor 100 this model forbids HOT updates outright, whereas real PostgreSQL will still use incidental free space on a page — the steady state on a packed table is the same.
