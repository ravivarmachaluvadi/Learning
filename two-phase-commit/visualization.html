The Commit Point

Move ₹500 from alice at Bank A to bob at Bank B — atomically, though the banks share nothing. Two-phase commit does it with one referee, two rounds of messages, and three durable logs. Every log is on screen: the protocol IS the logs.

The setup. alice has ₹1000 at Bank A; bob has ₹200 at Bank B. Phase 1: the coordinator asks each bank to promise it could commit (the bank locks the account and writes the promise to its own disk first). Phase 2: if every promise is in, the coordinator writes one decision to its disk — that single write is the commit — then tells everyone.

New here? Press “Run the whole story for me”: four acts — a clean transfer, a veto, the famous nightmare (coordinator dies after the promises; both banks freeze), and the same crash one instant earlier, ending the opposite way.

What will Bank B do when asked to promise?
Bank A always promises YES first — so you control the vote that arrives when a promise already exists.
Crashing the referee
“Next step” runs ONE action at a time. Crash the coordinator between any two — the deadliest spots are just before and just after it writes its decision. Its durable log survives every crash.
