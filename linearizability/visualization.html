The Single-Copy Illusion

A storage system with many machines wants you to believe there is only one copy of your data, and that every request happens at one instant. Linearizability is the name for that promise being kept. This page is the machine that decides whether it was.

Each bar below is one request: it starts when a client asked and ends when the client was told an answer. Nothing here shows what happened inside the system — that is the point. Drag the bars, change the answers, and see when the illusion holds.

This page models a single register — one key, holding one number. Real systems hold many, and the useful fact is that if every key is linearizable on its own, the whole store is linearizable together. That property is called locality, and it is why this is the guarantee real systems aim for.
