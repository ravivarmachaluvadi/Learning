Paxos Message Lab

Five teachers must agree on one destination for the school trip. They never speak to each other — you can only leave notes in their mailboxes, and notes can be delayed, lost or duplicated. Two class reps are both trying to book, and they want different places. You decide which note gets delivered next, so you can build the exact disaster you want and check whether the rules still hold.

Class reps (Paxos calls them proposers) want a destination booked. Rep 1 wants the Zoo, Rep 2 the Museum.
Teachers (acceptors) each keep two facts: the highest slot number they promised, and what they last wrote down.
A decision is made when 3 of the 5 teachers have written the same thing. After that it must never change.

Guided experiments — predict first, then run

  1. A clean run

    One rep, no failures, notes all arrive.
    Watch for: no teacher had written anything, so Rep 1 gets to use its own choice. Two phases, six notes, done.

  2. A decision can never be changed

    Rep 1 gets the Zoo decided by teachers A, B, C. Rep 2 then tries the Museum with a different majority, C, D, E.
    Watch for: teacher C is in both majorities. C reports the Zoo, and Rep 2 is forced to abandon the Museum and campaign for the Zoo instead. That overlap is the whole reason Paxos is safe — two groups of 3 out of 5 can never miss each other.

  3. "Some wrote it down" is not "decided"

    Rep 1's write-it-down notes reach only 2 of 5 teachers; the rest are lost.
    Watch for: the Museum wins — and that is correct, not a bug. Two out of five was never a decision, so nothing was overturned. Telling these two situations apart is the single most common Paxos misunderstanding.

  4. Two reps, endless duel

    Each rep keeps claiming a higher slot number just before the other can finish.
    Watch for: four rounds, nothing decided, nobody at fault, no messages lost. Paxos never decides wrongly, but it can fail to decide at all. Real systems fix this by picking one leader — not by changing the rules.

  5. The teacher who forgets

    Turn off "teachers write promises down", decide the Zoo, then reboot A, B and C.
    Watch for: two different decisions, and the safety light turning red. Every teacher followed the rules — but a teacher who forgets its own past is effectively lying about it. This is why real acceptors must flush their state to disk before replying.
