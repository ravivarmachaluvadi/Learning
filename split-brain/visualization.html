Two Signal Boxes

One stretch of single track. Two signal boxes, one at each end, joined by a telephone line. Only one box may let a train onto the track. When the phone line goes quiet, each signalman has to decide the one question he cannot answer: is the other box destroyed, or is the line simply down? Guess wrong in the cautious direction and no trains run. Guess wrong in the confident direction and two trains enter the same track from opposite ends.

Below, the signal boxes are servers, the telephone line is the network, and the single track is a shared database. Watch the two numbers on the right. They are not the same number, and confusing them is the whole subject.

What this simulator is and is not

The election here is Raft-shaped but deliberately simplified: a server that times out raises its epoch and asks for votes, and a vote is granted to any higher epoch. Real Raft also refuses to vote for a candidate whose log is behind, which is what protects already-committed entries — that check is not modelled, because this page is about authority and side effects rather than log repair. Message delivery is instant-ish (a fixed few ticks) and never lost; the only faults are the ones you can press. Everything you see here is produced by the rules on this page, not by a recording.
