No Orphans

A request handler forks several children (calls to other services) and needs their answers. The rule structured concurrency adds: the handler owns its children — a failure calls everyone home, and the handler cannot move on while any child is still out there. This page simulates one scope. Click a child card to make that child fail; use the story buttons if you're new — each run is narrated below.

Rules this page uses: all children are forked at t=0 (each on its own cheap virtual thread — see the java-virtual-threads lab); a child marked “fails” throws at 40% of its workload; “cancel” means the scope interrupts the child's thread, and a cooperative child stops ~5 ms later. In the old world the caller collects results in fork order and returns at the first failure it meets — nothing is ever cancelled, which is precisely the disease. The two timestamps in the table are the point: verdict is when the caller knew the outcome; all work ended is when the last thread actually stopped. Structured concurrency's promise is that for a scope those are the same moment — no work ever outlives the verdict.
