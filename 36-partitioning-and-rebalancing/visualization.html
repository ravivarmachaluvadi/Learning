The Moving Shelves — partitioning & rebalancing

Meera's library outgrew one building. Every book needs exactly one home a clerk can find, the queues should be even, and when a new building opens, whole shelves roll across town — while the library stays open. The Map is about where things belong; The Move is about how one shelf travels without losing a single book; The Recount is what you do when the shelf count itself turns out wrong; The Rules is what happens when a shelf is not allowed to live just anywhere. Start with the numbered scenarios: predict, then run.

Why these knobs?

Nothing here is hardcoded on purpose: the scheme, the shelf count P, the workload and the building count are all yours to change, because each one is a real decision someone made in every real system. The auto-split threshold is fixed at 30 books per shelf (real systems: ~512 MB–10 GB per partition) and splits happen at the shelf's median book.

What to look at

Each row is one record (a reader's address) with its version — every write bumps it. A green-tinted row on D means "copied". The question that decides everything: after the catalog flips to D, does D hold the latest version of every acknowledged write? The verdict bar keeps score. What to look at

Two numbers, and they are not the same number. Changed shelf counts books whose address changed — a bookkeeping fact. Crossed the network counts books whose bytes actually had to be carried to another building — the bill you pay in hours and bandwidth. A move can be huge on the first number and free on the second, or vice versa. Every button here changes exactly one of the two hops; watch which number reacts.

The rules of placement

Rules the placer must obey
Open a building

What to look at

Up to now the placer had one goal: keep the buildings even. Real ones have rules first and evenness second — don't put all three copies of a shelf in one rack (one power failure loses the lot), and don't move European readers' records out of Europe. Watch the status column: a shelf that cannot be placed legally is not placed at all. The system does not warn you by moving; it warns you by sitting still.
