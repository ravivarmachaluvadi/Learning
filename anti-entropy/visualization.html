Replica Repair Bench — three shops, one price list

Three bakeries keep their own copy of the same sixteen-item price list. Head office counts a price change as done once two shops have confirmed it — so a shop that was busy keeps selling at the old price, and nothing in the ordinary running of the business ever goes back to check. This page is that business, and the machinery that fixes it: leave a note, fix it when someone asks, or run a comparison.

The rules this simulator follows (so you can check that the numbers mean what the lab says they mean)
  • Three shops, A, B and C. Every shop holds every one of the sixteen items. A stored row is {timestamp, price, deleted}; a row can also be simply absent.
  • A write goes from the chosen shop to every shop it can reach, and is reported successful if at least W shops stored it. A shop that receives a write keeps it only if its timestamp beats what is already there — an older write loses on arrival.
  • For every shop it could not reach, the coordinating shop parks a hint (if hints are on). Hints are replayed when the target reopens, unless they are older than the hint window. A shop that closes loses every hint it was holding.
  • A read asks R shops, preferring the shop that took the request. It answers with the newest record among them, and (if read repair is on) writes that record back to the shops it compared.
  • A delete writes a headstone (tombstone) carrying its timestamp, unless headstones are turned off, in which case it removes the row. "Clear old headstones" is compaction: it discards headstones older than the grace period.
  • A Merkle repair builds a hash tree over the item list on each of the two shops, compares them from the root down, skips any branch whose two hashes match, and transfers every item inside each mismatched leaf range in both directions, resolving each with the merge rule.
  • Costs are counted as: 32 bytes per hash exchanged, 200 bytes per row streamed. "Rows read off disk" counts the full scan both shops do to build their trees, which happens whether or not anything is wrong.
  • Simplifications: every shop holds every item (no token ranges); repair is pairwise and instantaneous; hashing and streaming take no time; there is one datacentre.
