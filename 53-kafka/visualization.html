The Deodar Ledger · a Kafka simulator

Deodar Dispatch scans parcels all day. Four teams want the scans. This page is the ledger those scans are written into — four views of the same machine. Everything is a small, readable model of this lab's Kafka (single-digit records, three brokers), not a real client: it shows you the state machine, not the wire protocol.

Where does a record land, and who can read it?

Producers append parcel scans. The key is the parcel id, and the key alone decides the partition. Two consumer groups — notifications and analytics — read the same partitions at their own speed. Watch three things: a parcel's scans always stack in one column; reading never removes anything; and what happens to a parcel's story when you add a partition.

One partition, three copies: LEO, high watermark, ISR

This is a single partition with three replicas. LEO is how far a replica has got. The high watermark is the highest offset every in-sync replica holds — consumers may not read past it. The ISR is the set of replicas currently keeping up. Produce records, run replication rounds, freeze a follower, then kill the leader and see whether anything the producer was told was durable actually was.

The log is not infinite: deleting by clock, or keeping the latest per key

A partition is a row of segment files. Only the last one is active (being appended to); the rest are closed and immutable. Deletion removes whole closed segments — never single records. Switch the policy and watch what a consumer starting from the beginning would see afterwards.
