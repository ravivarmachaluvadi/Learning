The Price of Now

Three cities, real network delays, and the two questions a replicated system actually faces. Tab 1 asks how fresh an answer is — who waits, who gets refused, who is served a stale price. Tab 2 asks whether a rule can break — ten necklaces, and an eleventh sale that must be impossible.

One product price, three cities, real network delays. Pick one of five replication designs — one per PACELC class, plus one that sits between them — write and read from any city, send a client's reads through a different city, cut a region off, and watch who waits, who gets refused, who gets a stale answer, and who loses sight of their own change. Clients — each city has one

A write proposes the next price for the necklace (₹54,000, ₹58,000, …). A read asks “what is the price right now?”. Every read is judged by the recency rule: it must not return a value older than the newest write acknowledged before the read began.

Drag distance down to 0.1× to put the three replicas about 10 ms apart, the way three datacentres on one continent are: the majority design's write ack falls from 110 ms to 12 ms. “Consistency is slow” is partly a claim about your map, not about physics.
The failure detector everywhere is a 1000 ms timeout: a coordinator that waits that long gives up (refuses, or — in the bottom-right design — acknowledges without the missing region). Drag distance up and watch designs cross that line with no partition anywhere: the P in PACELC is a threshold you configure, not a physical event.
