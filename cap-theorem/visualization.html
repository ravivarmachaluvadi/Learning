CAP Partition Lab

One bank account, copied onto three machines. Cut the network between them and you hit the wall the CAP theorem describes: every request must either be refused (the answer stays correct, the customer gets nothing) or answered anyway (the customer gets served, the answer may be wrong). There is no third option. Cut the cable below and try to find one.

The account. Your balance starts at $100. Each machine (A, B, C) keeps its own copy — that's what "replica" means.
The customers. Two people share the account: L and R. Each is connected to one machine, and you choose which.
The storm. Cutting the network stops the machines from talking to each other. Each customer can still reach their own machine.

Guided experiments

  1. A normal day

    L deposits, then R checks the balance — network intact.
    Watch for: the deposit reaches all three machines, and R sees the new balance. Both correct and available. CAP costs you nothing until the network breaks.

  2. The storm, in CP mode

    Cut the network, then L and R each try to deposit and check.
    Watch for: L (alone with machine A) is refused both times — the machine would rather say nothing than risk a wrong answer. R still works, because B and C are a majority. Notice the outage is partial: it hits whoever is stuck on the small side.

  3. The storm, in AP mode

    Same cut, but now every machine answers with whatever it knows.
    Watch for: nobody is refused — and R's balance is wrong. That is the whole theorem in two clicks: the same storm, the other casualty.

  4. Both sides deposit, then the storm ends

    In AP mode both customers deposit during the cut, then the network heals.
    Watch for: the machines re-sync — and one deposit that the bank already confirmed is silently thrown away. AP doesn't cancel the pain; it postpones it to cleanup time.
