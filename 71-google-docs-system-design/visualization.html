Collaborative editing, one keystroke at a time

Two cursors, one sentence

The document says HAT. Alice wants to make it CHAT. Bob wants to make it HATS. They type at the same moment, on two computers, with a network in between. Everyone should end up reading CHATS — and getting that right is most of what a system like Google Docs does.

Nothing here moves on its own. You decide who types, and you decide when each message crosses the network.

Reading this screen

Waiting for confirmation is the edit a person has sent to the server and not heard back about. A client will only ever have one of those at a time; anything typed meanwhile queues up behind it. Revision is how many edits that copy has been told about — the server's revision number is the official count. Messages in flight are on the network right now: click Deliver to let one arrive, or Drop to have the network eat it. A message greyed out is waiting its turn, because everything sent to one person arrives in the order it was sent.
